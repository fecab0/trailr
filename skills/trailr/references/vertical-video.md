# Vertical video (9:16)

Don't crop the horizontal film; re-lay it out for a phone held in the hand.
- Canvas 1080×1920. 30fps is fine (60 for very kinetic pieces). 15–45s; for Reels/TikTok offer a ~30s cut.
- Safe zones for Reels/TikTok/Shorts: keep headlines, logo and CTA out of the top ~220px, the bottom ~400px (captions and UI) and the right ~140px (action buttons). Decorative color, blobs, clouds and props can bleed into those areas.
- Stack, don't split: headline in the upper third (~y 250–600, left-aligned or centered), one hero object in the middle (a phone mockup is the natural hero in vertical; laptops go smaller and tilted), supporting chips/stickers around it. Never the side-by-side headline + UI layout of 16:9. Two-up stat cards and 2-row chip grids work well.
- Type is bigger relative to width: headlines ~100–140px on 1080 width, 2–3 words per line, max 3–4 lines. UI text ~1.2× the horizontal sizes. Numbers can go 200px+.
- Hook in the first second: text on screen from frame 1 (e.g. the first word already rising, a typed line, a question, a flipping board), no slow fade from black. Cut roughly every 1.5–3s.
- Many people watch muted: every key message must be readable on screen; the soundtrack is a bonus.
- End card: logo + one-line promise + URL pill, held ≥2s, placed in the middle safe area; ideally the last frame loops cleanly back to the first.
- When both formats are requested: one timeline, one SFX track (so the soundtrack is shared); a second page or per-format CSS for layout, with per-format camera/guide keyframes. Review both contact sheets.

## Paid social ad for a service (9:16, text + music, no voiceover)

For an ad that must sell a service (someone comes and does something for you), not an app:
- Show the service happening: who arrives, what they do, how long. A plain before/after (empty → full) looks nice but the viewer does not understand what they are buying; this was the main feedback on a first cut.
- Use words the viewer already knows. Internal or brand nicknames for the people or the product go on the landing page, never in the ad; say "a cook", "a cleaner", "a tutor".
- State the outcome in the viewer's terms, not in units: "lunch and dinner, Monday to Friday" beats "10 portions"; a number can live in a small sticker. Check every claim (hours, quantities, price) against the landing page. Flag mismatches for the user before finalizing the script; editing the landing page requires a separate request.
- Rewind structure works well: open on the problem moment (Wednesday night, empty fridge), rewind fast (reverse motion, day counter running back), show the service day (doorbell, the person arrives with the groceries, a clock running through the hours while the work piles up), return to the problem moment now solved.
- Hold the payoff shot (the full fridge, the clean house) at least ~3 s: it sells more than a long end card. Then a short end card with the URL visible ≥1.5–2 s.
- Make two concepts to A/B test in the ads rather than polishing one. Price on screen is optional ("from 50 €" filters curious clicks).
- Never use AI-generated "real" photos (dishes, homes, people) in an ad: they read as real results. Flat illustration or real footage only.
- In the stills review, check character details at full size: props must sit right on the body (a chef hat centered on the head, touching it), not floating off-axis.
